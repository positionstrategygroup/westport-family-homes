When Ben Stiller Came to Stew Leonard's — And What It Says About Life in Westport, CT

Published September 2, 2026 · Westport Family Homes

There's a certain kind of Saturday that only really happens around here. You run out to Stew Leonard's in East Norwalk for milk, and the parking lot is fuller than usual. There's a line stretching past the entrance and it turns out Ben Stiller is inside meeting fans and pouring his new soda. That was the scene on Saturday, August 22, when Stiller stopped by the Norwalk location to promote Stiller's Soda, his low-sugar drink brand that launched last fall in Root Beer, Shirley Temple, and lemon-lime flavors.

View this post on Instagram

It wasn't Stiller's first stop at a Stew Leonard's for the soda — he'd made a similar appearance at the chain's Yonkers location back in December. But there was something fitting about this one landing in Norwalk, a few minutes from Westport, in a part of Connecticut where a genuine A-lister showing up at the grocery store isn't exactly shocking. Plenty of well-known names live in or pass through this stretch of Long Island Sound's Gold Coast.

Stew Leonard's Has Always Been More Than a Grocery Store

None of this happens by accident. Stew Leonard's built its reputation over decades by turning a grocery run into an event — animatronic characters, live music, seasonal festivals, and now, apparently, celebrity meet-and-greets. It's the kind of place that gives a Tuesday errand the energy of a Saturday outing, which is a big part of why it's stayed a fixture for local families for so long.

Stiller, for his part, is no stranger to reinventing himself between projects. Beyond the comedies he's best known for, he directed the first two seasons of "Severance," is producing an HBO documentary on the Knicks' championship run, and is still expanding the soda brand that brought him to Norwalk in the first place.

The Kind of Thing That Makes This Area What It Is

Moments like this are small, but they add up to something. Fairfield County doesn't need a tourism campaign — it has Compo Beach, The Westport Playhouse, the Westport Farmers' Market, a walkable Main Street, free summer concerts at the Levitt Pavilion, and, evidently, the occasional afternoon where a movie star is handing out soda samples at the store you already had on your list. It's the texture of ordinary weekends here that tends to surprise people who haven't spent real time in the area — and it's a big reason families who move here end up staying.
